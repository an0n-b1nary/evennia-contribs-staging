When the outcome of something your character tries is uncertain, the game makes a **check**: one of your stats against a difficulty or an opponent. The game decides your stat names and grades. This page covers what's the same in every game that uses these rules.

## Grades and pips

Each stat has a [[glossary-grade|grade]]. Games often use letter grades, but yours might use ranks or titles instead. A higher grade is better.

Within a grade, [[glossary-pip|pips]] fine-tune a stat. A `+` pip makes it a little stronger, and a `-` pip makes it a little weaker. A stat can carry both, so `B ++ -` shows two `+` pips and one `-` pip. Only the net count matters to the dice, but your sheet keeps both so the character reads the way you meant.

- **Pips usually taper.** Each extra `+` pip is typically worth a little less than the one before, and each extra `-` pip costs a little more. Pips can also count for less on the highest grades.
- **Pips never add up to a grade.** A stat with all the `+` pips it can hold is still weaker than the grade above, and one with all the `-` pips is still stronger than the grade below.

:::insight[Why pips never cross a grade]
A grade should mean the same thing on every sheet. If enough pips could lift a stat past the next grade, two characters with the same grade could be worlds apart, and the grade would stop telling you anything.
:::

Abilities and other effects can add bonuses on top. A bonus isn't a pip, so it can carry a stat past the next grade.

## Outcomes

Every check lands on one ladder of outcomes, worst to best, such as failure, partial success, success and critical success. Your game names the steps. The better your stat compared with the difficulty, the further up the ladder you're likely to land, but the dice always leave some room for surprise.

## What you can see

Some effects on a check are visible to everyone, some only to the side they belong to (a feint, say), and some only to staff. An estimate of your chances only counts what you can see, so it's exactly as good as what your character knows. The real roll counts everything.

Staff can always see the full record of a check, including the dice.
